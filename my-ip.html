What Is My IP Address? How to Check It and What It Reveals

Every device that reaches the internet does so through an IP address, a number that lets servers know where to send data back. Most of the time you never think about it. But when you are setting up a remote camera, troubleshooting a connection, whitelisting an office network or testing a VPN, you suddenly need to know what the outside world sees.

The What is my IP tool shows that in one step. Here is what it displays, how it gets the information, and what you should and should not read into it.

What your public IP address is

There are two kinds of IP address you may meet. Your device has a private address on your home or office Wi-Fi, often beginning with 192.168. Websites never see that. They see your public address, which belongs to your internet provider's network and is shared by your router on behalf of every device behind it.

The tool reports the public one. If you are on mobile data, it is typically an address of your mobile carrier, and it can change when you reconnect or move between networks.

How to use the tool, step by step

  1. Open the page. A loading message, Fetching network data..., appears briefly.
  2. Read the large card labelled Your Public IP Address.
  3. Click Copy IP to copy the address; the button briefly changes to Copied!.
  4. Scan the boxes below for Location, Internet Provider, Country and Timezone.
  5. Use Refresh Data after switching Wi-Fi, mobile data or a VPN, to look up the new details.

When knowing your IP is useful

  • Remote access setups: confirming the address to allow in a server firewall or a cloud dashboard.
  • VPN testing: checking that the address and country change after you connect.
  • Support calls: giving your provider or an IT helper the address they ask for.
  • Comparing networks: seeing how your home broadband and your phone hotspot appear differently.
  • Region-locked content: checking which country a site is likely to think you are in.

How the lookup works, and a privacy note

Unlike most tools on this site, this one cannot work purely inside your browser, because your browser does not know its own public address. When the page loads, it sends a request to a third-party IP lookup service, ipapi.co, which replies with your IP and related details. So that service does see your address, as any website you visit does, and its own privacy terms apply to it.

The page states that it does not store your IP address on our servers. The results are shown only to you and are not saved by the page, but the lookup itself does involve the external service.

Reading the location data sensibly

The location is an estimate drawn from databases that map address ranges to places. It is usually accurate to the country and often to the region or city, but it is not a street address. Mobile users in particular can appear in a different city, because carriers route traffic through central points.

The Internet Provider field shows the organisation that owns the address range. If you use a VPN, it will show the VPN company rather than your own provider. This is normal and a sign that the VPN is working.

Troubleshooting

Error loading data. The page suggests disabling your ad-blocker or refreshing. Privacy extensions and some DNS filters block requests to lookup services. Try pausing them for this page, or switch network.

The location is wrong. The databases are estimates. Mismatch is common with mobile networks and with ISPs that serve a wide area.

The address keeps changing. Many Indian home and mobile connections use dynamic or shared addresses. If you need a fixed address for work, ask your provider about a static IP.

Rate limits. Free lookup services limit requests, so if you refresh many times in a short period the lookup may fail temporarily.

Limits and what an IP cannot tell you

An IP address does not reveal your name, phone number or exact home. The tool also does not check whether your IP is blacklisted, scan open ports, detect leaks or test connection speed. It shows your IPv4 or IPv6 address, whichever your connection uses, and nothing about DNS. For deeper network diagnostics, you need dedicated testing tools. For a quick look at what a website sees, this is the fast route.

Finally, be careful about posting your address publicly. It is not a password, but there is rarely a reason to share it widely.

Frequently asked questions

Is the My IP Address tool on AllToolsPro free to use?

Yes. My IP Address is completely free on AllToolsPro. There is no registration, no signup and no watermark, and it works on mobile phones and computers.

Does this tool send my IP to another service?

Yes. It uses the public lookup service ipapi.co to get your address and location details, so that service receives the request. The page says it does not store your IP on its own servers.

Why is my location shown in the wrong city?

IP location is an estimate based on address-range databases. Mobile carriers and some broadband providers can make you appear in a nearby or even distant city.

Why does my IP change?

Many providers assign dynamic addresses, and mobile networks share addresses across users. Reconnecting or switching networks can give you a new one.

Why do I see an error instead of my IP?

Ad-blockers, privacy extensions or network filters can block the lookup request. Disable them for the page or try another network, then refresh.

Does a VPN change what this tool shows?

Yes. With a VPN on, the address, provider and location usually reflect the VPN server rather than your own connection.
