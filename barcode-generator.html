Barcode Generator Guide: Choosing the Right Format and Printing It Properly

A barcode is just a pattern of bars that a scanner turns back into a number or short string. Shopkeepers use them to speed up billing, warehouses use them to track cartons, and clinics use them on sample labels. What trips people up is not drawing the bars but choosing the right format and getting the input exactly right.

This guide walks through the Barcode Generator: the seven formats it offers, the data each one accepts, how to download a clean PNG, and how to print it so it actually scans.

Why choosing the format matters

Every barcode format has its own rules about length and allowed characters. A scanner does not care how good your barcode looks; it cares that the symbol follows the standard. Pick the wrong format and the tool will flag the data as invalid.

The generator offers: CODE128 (standard), EAN-13 (product), UPC (retail), CODE39, ITF-14, MSI and Pharmacode. For internal use such as inventory, asset tags and order numbers, CODE128 is the easiest choice because it accepts letters, numbers and common symbols.

How to create a barcode, step by step

  1. Type your value into the Enter Data box. The preview updates as you type, so there is no generate button to press.
  2. Choose a type from the Format menu.
  3. If you like, change Bar Color. Keep it dark.
  4. Use Show Text Below Barcode? to display or hide the human-readable number under the bars.
  5. Check that no red error message says the format is invalid.
  6. Click Download PNG to save the image.

The tool loads with 12345678 as sample data, so you can see how a code looks before replacing it.

What each format expects

  • CODE128: Letters, numbers and symbols. Good for SKUs, shipment IDs and library accession numbers.
  • EAN-13: A 13-digit retail code. The tool expects 12 or 13 digits; when you give 12, the final check digit is normally calculated for you.
  • UPC: The 12-digit code used on retail goods in North America.
  • CODE39: Capital letters, digits and a few symbols. Lowercase letters will not work.
  • ITF-14: A numeric format used on outer cartons and cases, which takes 13 or 14 digits.
  • MSI: Digits only, seen in some inventory and shelf-labelling systems.
  • Pharmacode: A number-only code used in pharmaceutical packaging.

If the red warning appears, compare your input against these rules first.

Practical uses, including India-specific ones

A small retailer can label loose items or racks with CODE128 codes and scan them at the billing counter. A coaching institute can print ID cards with student numbers. A home business can put order numbers on parcels before handing them to a courier.

One important caution: an EAN-13 you generate here is simply a valid pattern. It does not give you ownership of that number. Products sold through large retail chains or online marketplaces normally need numbers allotted by the official global numbering body, which in India is GS1 India. Use made-up EAN or UPC values only for internal or practice purposes, because they can clash with someone else's real product.

Tips for a barcode that scans every time

  • Keep strong contrast. Dark bars on a white background scan best, and pale colours such as yellow or light grey often fail.
  • Leave blank space around the code, called the quiet zone. Do not let text or borders touch the bars.
  • Do not stretch the PNG unevenly. Resize it proportionally, and avoid shrinking it so far that the thin bars blur together.
  • Print at a sharp setting on a laser or good inkjet printer. Smudged thermal printing is a common cause of scan failures.
  • Always test a printed sample with the scanner or phone app you will use, before printing a whole sheet of labels.

Common mistakes and troubleshooting

Invalid format message. This usually means the wrong length or characters, such as letters in an EAN-13 or lowercase in CODE39.

The check digit looks different from what I typed. For retail formats the last digit is a calculation on the earlier digits. A wrong check digit means an invalid barcode, so let the tool generate it where it can.

The download is empty. The tool will warn you if there is no barcode. Enter valid data first.

It scans on screen but not on paper. Increase the printed size and check the contrast and quiet zone.

CODE128 is usually the best choice because it supports letters, numbers and symbols and is read by almost every scanner.
