How to Extract Specific Pages from a PDF, Including Non-Consecutive Ones

Sometimes you need page 3, page 17 and pages 40 to 44 of a document, and nothing else. Sending the whole thing is wasteful, and it may expose pages you would rather keep private.

Extracting pages creates a fresh PDF containing only your selection. Below is how the Extract PDF Pages tool works, how to write the page list correctly, and where it fits among other PDF tools.

What extracting pages means

Extraction copies the pages you name into a new PDF and leaves the original untouched. Unlike a simple start-to-end split, you can choose pages that are scattered across the document and combine them in a single output file.

It is the right choice whenever you know which pages you want to keep and the rest are irrelevant to the person receiving the file.

How to extract pages, step by step

  1. Click the Choose PDF File area, or drag and drop your PDF.
  2. Wait for the line that says "Document Ready" with the file name and page count.
  3. In the box under Specify pages to extract, type your selection. Use commas to separate pages and dashes for ranges, for instance 1, 4, 8-10.
  4. Click Extract & Download PDF.
  5. The new file downloads as Extracted_Pages_AllToolsPro.pdf.

Writing the page list correctly

The format is forgiving but literal. Numbers are separated by commas, ranges use a dash, and spaces are fine. Page numbers outside the document are ignored, and duplicates are counted once. Ranges written backwards, such as 10-8, are treated as 8-10.

One detail to remember: the pages in your output follow their original order in the source file, not the order you typed them. Entering 9, 2 gives you page 2 followed by page 9. If you need a custom order, run the result through a page organiser afterwards.

Where this is handy

  • Bank and loan paperwork: Share only the pages with the relevant account summary instead of a full statement.
  • Exam preparation: Save the pages of a syllabus, previous-year paper or reference book that apply to you.
  • Government forms: Pick the filled application page and the declaration from a longer scanned bundle.
  • Business documents: Send a client just the pricing page and terms from a long proposal.

Tips for best results

  • Check page numbers in your PDF viewer, using its page counter rather than the number printed on the page.
  • Extract in one go rather than several runs, so you end up with a single file.
  • Open the output and confirm each page is present before sharing it.
  • If the extracted file is still large, compress it afterwards.

Common mistakes and fixes

"No valid pages found in your selection." Every number you typed was outside the document, or the text was not in the supported format. Remove stray letters and check the total page count.

"Invalid PDF file." The file may be damaged or not a real PDF. Try a repair tool, or re-download the original.

Missing pages in the output. A typo such as a full stop instead of a comma can cause a number to be skipped. Check your list carefully.

A worked example makes the format clear. Suppose a 60-page PDF holds a company's annual report and you need the cover (page 1), the summary (pages 3 to 5) and the contact page (page 60). You would type 1, 3-5, 60 and click the button. The new file holds five pages in that order.

If you instead type 60, 1, 3-5, the output is identical, because pages follow their original sequence. That is worth knowing if you expected the contact page to come first.

It also pays to extract once and then rename the file straight away. A folder with several files all called Extracted_Pages quickly becomes confusing, so add a short label before saving it alongside your other documents.

Privacy

Extraction runs in your browser, and the page explains that nothing is sent to its servers. That is useful when you are trimming a document specifically to avoid sharing its sensitive pages. Remember that the new file contains exactly what you selected, so double-check that nothing private slipped in.

When to use something else

This tool keeps pages; it does not shuffle them into a new order, rotate them or edit their content. To remove a few unwanted pages instead, a remove-pages tool is faster, because you list what to drop rather than what to keep. To rearrange, use a visual organiser. Very large files may be slow, since the document is processed in your device's memory. It also cannot open password-protected documents.

Finally, remember that extraction is a good privacy habit. Before emailing a long document, ask whether the recipient needs every page. Sending only the pages that matter reduces the chance of sharing unrelated personal or commercial details by accident, and it keeps attachments small.

Treat the extracted file as a new document in its own right: name it clearly, check it once, and file it where you can find it again.
