Meta Tag Analyzer: A Quick Health Check for Your Page's Head Section

By AllToolsPro · Updated 12 October 2026 · 6 min read

Before anyone clicks your page in search results, they see a title and a short description. When someone shares your link on WhatsApp or LinkedIn, they see a preview card. All of that comes from tags sitting in the head of your HTML, and they are easy to get wrong or forget entirely.

The Meta Tag Analyzer reads the head code you paste and checks a handful of the most important tags. This guide explains how to use it, what its score means, and what it deliberately does not check.

What the tool checks

The analyzer inspects five items: the Page Title, the Meta Description, Meta Keywords, the Robots Tag and the Open Graph Title. Each appears as a card marked PASS, WARN or FAIL, and the combined result is shown as an SEO Meta Score out of 100, based on tag presence and length.

The score is a simple checklist result. It is useful for catching obvious gaps, but it is not a prediction of rankings.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Open your web page, right-click and choose View Page Source. On a phone browser, you can type view-source: before the page address, or use your CMS's code view.
  2. Copy the content inside the head section, or the whole page if that is easier.
  3. Paste it into the box labelled Paste HTML Code (Header Section).
  4. Click Analyze Meta Tags.
  5. Review each card, read the length hint underneath, fix the tags in your site, and run the analysis again.

Reading the results

For the title, the tool treats a length from about 10 up to 59 characters as a pass. For the description, anything over 50 and under 160 characters passes, and the hint suggests aiming for roughly 120 to 160. Missing or very short or long values get a warning or fail.

The keywords and robots tags earn a PASS for simply being present. Open Graph title is a pass if it exists. Remember that length limits are guidelines about how much search results typically display, and search engines may rewrite what they show. A title or description that is slightly outside the range is not a disaster if it reads well.

Writing better titles and descriptions

  • Title: put the main topic first, make it specific and keep it unique per page. Avoid repeating the same title across pages.
  • Description: write one or two natural sentences that tell the searcher what they will get. It does not directly improve rankings, but a clear one can improve clicks.
  • Open Graph title: this controls the headline in link previews on social apps and messengers. It can be a little more conversational than the search title.
  • Robots: add it only when you need to give instructions such as noindex. Having no robots tag is normal and does not stop indexing.

Tips and common mistakes

Meta keywords are not important. Major search engines do not use them for ranking, so a WARN for missing keywords is not something to worry about. Do not stuff the tag.

Pasting only part of the head. If the tool says "Not Found" for a tag you know exists, check that the pasted code includes it and that quotes and attribute names are correct.

Checking the wrong page. Home page tags are often different from inner page tags. Analyze each important page separately.

Treating 100/100 as finished. A full score only means the five tags are present with acceptable lengths.

Privacy and what it does not cover

The pasted code is parsed in your browser. The tool does not fetch your live website, so it only sees what you paste. Avoid pasting pages with private tokens or admin-only data.

It does not check canonical tags, viewport settings, Twitter card tags, the Open Graph image or description, structured data, hreflang, page speed, content quality or links. For a complete audit, you will need those checks too. Use this tool as a fast first pass when writing or editing a page's head.

Frequently asked questions

Does the tool fetch my website automatically?

No. You paste your page's HTML, usually the head section, and the tool analyzes that code in your browser.

What title and description lengths pass?

A title between roughly 10 and 59 characters passes, and a description between about 50 and 159 characters passes. The tool's hint suggests aiming for 120 to 160 for descriptions.

Do I need the meta keywords tag?

No. Major search engines do not use it for ranking, so a missing keywords tag is not a problem.

Is a score of 100 a guarantee of good rankings?

No. The score only reflects whether five tags are present and within length limits. Rankings depend on many other factors.

Which tags are not checked?

Canonical, viewport, Twitter cards, Open Graph image and description, structured data and hreflang are not analyzed by this tool.
