How to Convert JPG Images to a PDF Without Installing Anything

By AllToolsPro · Updated 12 October 2026 · 5 min read

Most online forms do not want five separate photos of your certificate. They want one PDF. Whether it is a scholarship application, a bank KYC upload or an assignment submission, the portal usually accepts a single document, and your phone has probably saved everything as JPG.

The JPG to PDF tool on AllToolsPro solves that in a few taps. You add your pictures, arrange them, choose a page size and download one PDF. This guide walks through the real options on the page, shows where the tool shines, and is upfront about what it does not do.

What this tool does

It takes one or more JPEG images and places each one on its own PDF page. Each image is scaled to fit the page without stretching and is centred, so a tall photo and a wide photo both look natural. The result is a normal PDF that opens in any reader on a phone, laptop or printer.

It only accepts JPG and JPEG files. If your picture is a PNG or a screenshot saved in another format, convert it to JPG first or the tool will quietly skip it.

Step-by-step: from photos to PDF

  1. Open the tool and tap the box that says Drag & Drop images here, or drop your files on it. You can select several images at once.
  2. Check the thumbnails that appear. Drag any thumbnail to a new position to change the page order, and use the small remove button on a thumbnail to delete a wrong image.
  3. Set Page Size to A4, Letter or Legal. A4 is the default and the usual choice in India.
  4. Choose Orientation, Portrait or Landscape. This applies to every page of the PDF.
  5. Pick a Margin: No margin, Small margin or Large margin.
  6. Press Convert to PDF. The file downloads as AllToolsPro_Converted.pdf.

If you want to start over, Clear All removes every image at once.

Where people use it

  • Government and exam portals: Combine the front and back of an Aadhaar-style ID card or a set of marksheets into one upload.
  • Job applications: Merge certificates and experience letters into a single attachment for a recruiter.
  • Students: Turn photographed handwritten notes or assignment pages into one file for a teacher or class group.
  • Freelancers and shop owners: Bundle bill and receipt photos for a client or an accountant.
  • Everyday life: Put property papers or medical reports in one shareable file.

Tips for a clean result

  • Arrange the order before converting. Changing it afterwards means starting again.
  • Pick No margin for full-page photos and Small margin for documents you may print, so nothing is clipped by a printer.
  • Match orientation to your images. If most are landscape, choose Landscape so they fill the page instead of sitting small in the middle.
  • Photograph documents in good light and straighten them first, because the tool does not crop or rotate.

Common problems and fixes

An image did not appear

Only JPG and JPEG files are accepted. Check the file extension and format.

A photo looks sideways

The tool does not rotate images. Phone photos can carry a hidden rotation flag that some software ignores, so rotate the picture in your gallery app and save it before adding it.

The PDF is too large to upload

The images are embedded as they are, so a stack of high-resolution phone photos can make a heavy file. Resize the photos first, or run the finished PDF through a PDF compressor.

Choosing page size and margins

A4 measures 210 by 297 mm and is what Indian offices, colleges and most government portals expect. Letter is common in the United States, and Legal is a longer page used for contracts and some court papers. If you are unsure, stay with A4.

Margins matter more than people expect. With no margin, a photo can run to the very edge, which looks good on screen but may be trimmed by a home printer. A small margin leaves a thin white frame, and a large one gives a more formal, document-like look. Because every image is fitted inside the usable area, a bigger margin simply makes the picture a little smaller; it never crops it.

Privacy and security

The conversion is done by your browser, using the page itself. According to the tool page, your files are not uploaded to a server, which makes it a reasonable choice for ID photos and certificates. As with any sensitive document, only use a device you trust and delete the downloaded copy once you have submitted it.

Limits and when to use something else

This is a simple image-to-PDF converter. There is no per-page orientation, no cropping, no text recognition and no password protection. If you need searchable text from a scan, you need an OCR tool. If you need to combine existing PDFs, a merge tool is the better fit. For quick, tidy, image-only PDFs, though, it is hard to beat for speed.

Frequently asked questions

Can I convert PNG images with this tool?

No. The tool accepts JPG and JPEG files only. Convert PNG or other formats to JPG first, then add them.

Can I change the order of pages after adding images?

Yes. Drag the thumbnails into the order you want before pressing Convert to PDF. You can also remove individual images.

Which page sizes are supported?

A4, Letter and Legal, in Portrait or Landscape. You can also choose no margin, a small margin or a large margin.

Are my photos uploaded anywhere?

The tool page states that files are processed in your browser and are not uploaded to a server.

Is there a limit on how many images I can add?

The page does not state a fixed limit. Very large batches of high-resolution photos depend on your device memory, so convert in smaller groups if the browser slows down.
